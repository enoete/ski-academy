📓 Ninti's notebook
Floodplain
Flat land bordering the river banks
Silt
Fine soil the river leaves behind
Fertile
Good for growing crops
Semiarid
Under 10 inches of rain a year; hot summers
Drought
A long time with little or no rain and snow
Erosion / deposition
Water picks soil up / drops it off
Irrigation
Canals carry river water to fields (by about 6000 B.C.)
Water tools
Canals, silt-cleaning crews, dams, and the shaduf (a pole with a bucket on one end and a weight on the other that lifts river water, like a seesaw)
By 4000 B.C.
Farming villages all over southern Mesopotamia
Lock it in, the Goldilocks river: too little water = drought, too much = a violent flood, just right = silt and a big harvest. And nobody knew when: April? May? June? The fixes: “Dig it, Clean it, Block it, Lift it”: dig canals, clean out silt, block floods with dams, lift water with a shaduf.

📓 Ninti's notebook
Sumer
The first civilization, in southern Mesopotamia, around 3000 B.C.
First cities
Ur, Uruk and Eridu (also Kish, Nippur, Lagash). They controlled the lower part of the Tigris and Euphrates valleys.
City-state
A city that governs itself and its surrounding territory
Why independent?
Mud-flats and desert cut cities off from each other
Size
5,000–20,000 people, each with its own government
Layout
Big mud wall; gates open by day, shut at night; palace and ziggurat in the center
War & peace
Fought over resources and borders; in peace, traded and formed alliances
Civilization
Cities & government, religion, social structure, writing & art
Lock it in, “Can Royals Sing Well?” Cities & government, Religion, Social structure, Writing & art. If a test asks for two features of Sumer that made it a civilization, name two with an example, like “writing (cuneiform) and religion (ziggurats).”

Cheat sheet

Geography
  • Mesopotamia = land between the rivers = Fertile Crescent
  • Tigris (east) & Euphrates (west)
  • Start in the mountains of Turkey & Kurdistan; flow southeast through Iraq to the Persian Gulf
  • Borders: Zagros Mts, Syrian & Arabian Deserts, Persian Gulf
  • Southwest Asia = the Middle East
  • Nineveh to Ur ≈ 400 miles
River words
  • Floodplain, silt, fertile, semiarid (under 10 in.), drought
  • Erosion picks up soil; deposition drops it
  • Floods unpredictable: April to June
  • Irrigation canals, silt crews, dams, shaduf
Resources
  • No forests, stone or metals
  • Mud bricks & plaster (crumbled easily)
  • Few natural barriers, so mud walls
  • Surplus grain traded for stone, wood, metal
  • Community leaders organized the work
City-states & civilization
  • Sumer: first civilization, ~3000 B.C.
  • Ur, Uruk, Eridu (also Kish, Nippur, Lagash)
  • Cut off by mud-flats & desert, so independent
  • 5,000–20,000 people; own government; big wall
  • War over resources; trade & alliances in peace
  • Can Royals Sing Well?
Religion & rulers
  • Polytheism; a patron god for each city
  • Ziggurat = “to rise high” (Akkadian)
  • Top: only special priests
  • Priests → war-hero kings → hereditary (monarchy)
  • Power “from the city's god”
People & trade
  • Upper: kings, priests, warriors, officials
  • Middle (largest): merchants, farmers, fishers, artisans
  • Lowest: enslaved people
  • Carnelian (red, Indus Valley), lapis lazuli (blue, Afghanistan)
  • Write, Roll, Sail, Plow, Spin, Melt, Count
