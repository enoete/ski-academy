Level 1

Earth's food factories

⭐star

Producers make their own food using sunlight. Plants, algae and certain bacteria are producers. They are also called autotrophs, which means "self-feeders". They don't hunt or shop for food. They make it! This happens through photosynthesis: green leaves use sunlight, air and water to make glucose (a sugar that is food for the plant).

Level 3

Arrow detective

⭐star

The arrows are the most important part of any food chain or web, and the easiest marks to lose. Learn the four arrow rules from class.

1An arrow means "is eaten by". Grass → rabbit means grass is eaten by the rabbit.
2The arrowhead points to the animal receiving the energy.
3Arrows show how food energy moves along the chain.
4Energy never travels backward. It only flows one way.
Never point the arrow at what is being eaten!The arrow always points to the hungry feeder that receives the energy.

Level 5

Chain vs web

⭐star

A single food chain is one path: grass → caterpillar → blue tit → hawk. A food web connects many overlapping food chains, like a real, resilient community. Most animals have varied diets: they eat more than one kind of organism, which gives them backup food options.

Level 6

Closing the loop

⭐star

What happens when plants shed leaves or animals die? Decomposers like fungi, bacteria and earthworms break down dead plants and animals. They return nutrients (the things living things need to grow) to the soil. These returned nutrients enrich the soil, so producers can sprout and grow once again.

Level 7

The mosquito case file

⭐star
🦟

Everyone wants mosquitoes gone. But scientists say they have ecological roles too. Mosquitoes help the environment as pollinators, as a food source for wildlife, and by helping with nutrient recycling. Their larvae live in water, where they filter the water and eat decaying algae and bacteria. Larvae and adults are food for fish, tadpoles and turtles. But mosquitoes also carry dangerous viruses, parasites and other pathogens, which they pass to humans and animals through their saliva when they bite.
