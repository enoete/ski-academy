2

Nature gives us everything

Biodiversity is not just "nice to have". People depend on it every day in three big ways. Learn these three; the test will ask you to explain them.

Food & nutrition

Crops, fruits and fish rely on wild genetic variety and animal pollinators like bees and hummingbirds. Wild relatives of crops carry genes that can make farm crops survive drought or disease.

Medicine & health

About half of modern medicines came from, or were inspired by, compounds first found in wild plants, fungi and other living things. Penicillin came from a mould. Aloe soothes burns. Every lost species could be a lost cure.

Useful materials

Timber, cotton, rubber, bamboo and plant fibres give us shelter and clothing. Your school uniform and the wood in your house both started as living things.

Two places, one big idea

Local · St Kitts

Central Forest Reserve

A tropical forest on the mountains that gives habitat to native plants, birds, monkeys and other wildlife. It shows that even a small island ecosystem can hold lots of biodiversity.

Global · South America

Amazon Basin

A huge rainforest holding millions of living organisms. It affects climate and water cycles far beyond its own borders, so what happens there matters to the whole world.

The web of life

Plants make food, pollinators help plants reproduce, predators keep populations in check, and decomposers return nutrients to the soil. Tap a species to remove it and watch what happens to everyone connected to it.

Lost a food source but has a backupIn danger: no backup left

Every species is healthy. Try removing the caterpillar first, then reset and remove the bee.

The big lessonSpecies with more than one food source or helper (like the small bird) survive a loss. Species with only one (like the frog) are in trouble. More biodiversity means more backups.

What disrupts ecosystems?

A disruption is a sudden or major change that harms an ecosystem. Some come from nature. Some come from people.

Natural causes

🌀 Hurricanes  ☀️ Droughts  🌊 Floods
🔥 Wildfires  🌋 Volcanic eruptions

Caribbean link: hurricanes can flatten mangroves and break coral reefs.

Human causes

🧴 Pollution  🪓 Deforestation
🐟 Invasive species  🎣 Overfishing

Caribbean link: lionfish were released by people into Atlantic waters and now eat huge numbers of young reef fish.

Why it mattersA disruption can remove food, shelter or breeding places. The damage spreads through the web, in coral reefs, grasslands, forests, lakes, wetlands and even city parks.

Story: the parrotfish problem

Parrotfish graze on algae that grows over coral, like lawnmowers on a reef. Tap each step to see the chain reaction when a reef is overfished.

Resilience: bouncing back

Resilience is the ability of an ecosystem to recover after a disruption and return to a healthy state. Think of a sports team with a full bench: if one player gets hurt, someone else steps in. Run the experiment below.

Answer like a scientist

Your test uses application questions with short answers. A strong short answer has three ingredients. Use this recipe every time.

1. Say it

Claim. Answer the question directly in one sentence.

2. Science it

Reason. Explain why, using a key word: biodiversity, habitat, resilience, ecosystem.

3. Show it

Example. Give a real effect or example: fish, bees, soil, storms.

Question: Why does a forest with many tree species recover faster after a disease?

A forest with many species recovers faster because the disease usually attacks only one species, so the other species survive and keep the ecosystem working. The surviving trees still hold the soil together and give birds and monkeys shelter while new trees grow.
