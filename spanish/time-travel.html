Spanish Time Travel:
Past, Present & Future

Verbs are time travellers. In Spanish, a verb changes the end of the word to tell you when something happens and who is doing it. You'll learn the trick and compare it with English, then finish with a big quiz.

⏳ What is a "tense"?

A tense is the time of an action. Did it already happen? Is it happening now? Will it happen later? Every sentence you say has a time hidden inside its verb (the action word).

🌙

PAST · el pasado

Already happened.

Yesterday I danced.

☀️

PRESENT · el presente

Happening now, or happens regularly.

Today I dance.

🚀

FUTURE · el futuro

Hasn't happened yet.

Tomorrow I will dance.

🇬🇧 ↔ 🇪🇸 How English and Spanish show time

Both languages have past, present and future, so you already know the idea. The difference is how they show the time.

🚂 The train story

Picture a verb as a little train. English usually adds a new carriage in front: will dance. Spanish changes the last carriage instead: bailaré. All the information about time and person rides at the back of the train.

Time🇬🇧 English does this…🇪🇸 Spanish does this…
PresentShe dances (small change)Ella baila
PastShe danced (adds -ed)Ella bailó
Futurewill dance (adds a helper word in front)Ella bailará (no helper word, just a new ending)

💡 One word, three meanings

Hablo can mean I speak, I am speaking, OR I do speak. English needs three versions. Spanish uses one word.

💡 Spanish can skip "I"

The ending already says who it is, so Spanish often drops the pronoun. Bailo = I dance. In English you can't just say "Dance" and mean "I dance".

💡 English has rule-breakers too

English: eat → ate, run → ran, sing → sang (no -ed!). Spanish also has some rule-breakers, but the verbs here follow the rules: comí (I ate).

🙋🏽‍♀️ The "WHO" part: pronouns

The ending tells you the time and also who is doing it. These are the "who" words. They line up with English almost exactly.

🧩 The two parts of a verb

Every Spanish verb in its basic form (the infinitive, meaning "to ___") ends in -ar, -er, or -ir. These are the three verb families.

hablSTEM (the meaning)+arENDING (who + when)

-AR family

hablar (to speak)
bailar (to dance)
cantar (to sing)

-ER family

comer (to eat)
correr (to run)
beber (to drink)

-IR family

vivir (to live)
escribir (to write)
abrir (to open)

🍰 The recipe: Chop off the -ar / -er / -ir to get the stem, then add the ending you need. For the future there's a shortcut: you keep the whole word and add the ending to the end.

🟢 Present tense · el presente

Use it for what is happening now, or things you do regularly ("I eat breakfast every day").

Recipe: stem + present ending

🎵 Memory song: For -AR, the vowel is A: o, as, a, amos, áis, an. For -ER and -IR, the vowel becomes E: o, es, e… -ER and -IR only differ for nosotros (comemos / vivimos) and vosotros.

🟣 Past tense · el pretérito

Use it for something that happened and finished, like "Yesterday I sang." It does the same job as English -ed.

Recipe: stem + past ending

⚠️ Accent marks matter!

hablo = I speak (now)
habló = he/she spoke (past)

The little mark (´) changes the time and the person. Always watch for it.

🎉 Good news

-ER and -IR verbs use the exact same past endings: í, iste, ió, imos, isteis, ieron.

🤔 Twin words

hablamos = "we speak" AND "we spoke"! Same for vivimos. Clue words like ayer (yesterday) tell you which one it means.

🟠 Future tense · el futuro

Use it for things that will happen. English says will + verb. Spanish puts the "will" into the ending.

Recipe (shortcut!): whole infinitive + future ending. Don't chop anything off.

bailarkeep the WHOLE word+é= "I will"=bailaré

🌟 Easiest tense

The future endings are the same for -AR, -ER and -IR: é, ás, á, emos, éis, án.

🎁 Bonus: "going to"

English: I'm going to sing. Spanish does almost the same thing: voy a cantar.

voy a / vas a / va a / vamos a / van a + infinitive. Very common, and very close to English!

👀 Sneak peek: rule-breakers (irregular verbs)

English has "go → went". Spanish has its own rule-breakers. You'll meet more later, but here are a few common ones:

VerbPresentPastFuture

tener (to have)tengo = I havetuve = I hadtendré = I will have
hacer (to do/make)hago = I dohice = I didharé = I will do
